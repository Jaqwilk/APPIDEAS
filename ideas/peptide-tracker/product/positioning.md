# Positioning and differentiation

Status: proposed from the research, awaiting validation.

## Audience and job

Initial hypothesis: adults managing several user-entered schedules and containers who use notes/spreadsheets or struggle with current tools. The job is to keep an accurate personal record of actual use and associated supply without extensive setup.

Working promise: **“Know what you recorded, when, and how much is left.”** No final brand name has been selected.

## Three mechanisms

| Mechanism | User-visible proof | Evidence basis |
|---|---|---|
| One record updates history and the selected container | Visible usage amount, source container, undo and linked correction | PeptidePal supply complaints; Pep AI inventory praise; Shotsy refill requests |
| The routine stays easy | Short setup, quick record, stable navigation | Onboarding complaints, Pep AI redesign reaction, Shotsy simplicity praise |
| History remains under user control | Tested restore, export and access after Pro ends | Save/migration/access complaints |

These are proposed execution advantages, not exclusive features no competitor has. Demonstrate them in usability tests and screenshots instead of claiming to be the most advanced tracker.

## Differentiation by competitor

- PeptidePal: clearer first use, easier correction and explainable supply rather than another large library.
- Pep AI: stable task layout and optional additions rather than compulsory redesign/companion/feed.
- PepCalc: persistent personal records beyond a one-off arithmetic task; retain its narrow-task simplicity.
- Shotsy: supply-linked multi-schedule workflow and portable detailed history; basic GLP-1 reminders/graphs/PDF already compete with an established free core.

## Product principles

1. Actual records and future plans are separate.
2. Every quantity shows its unit and meaning.
3. Corrections are reversible and propagate consistently.
4. A save succeeds locally before success is shown.
5. Users retain their history regardless of subscription status.
6. Extra modules cannot obstruct the main task.

## Marketing examples to test

- “Record use and see your supply update.”
- “Correct an entry without rebuilding your schedule.”
- “Your history stays yours. Export it when you need it.”

Avoid medical-outcome guarantees, fabricated user counts and claims of market exclusivity. Do not promote unapproved suppliers or portray educational AI as a personal dosing adviser.
