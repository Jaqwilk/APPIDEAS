# Validation and delivery plan

No effort estimate or calendar commitment has been approved. Stages are ordered by evidence and dependency, not a promise of a fixed release date.

## Stage 1 — discovery and prototype

Interview 15–20 people, separating conventional GLP-1 tracking from complex multi-schedule/supply use. Ask about the last real failure, current workaround and reason for selecting/paying for an existing tool. Test a prototype with 8–10 target users. Do not ask only whether they like the idea.

Tasks: configure a real routine, record use, correct a wrong date/amount/container, reconcile remaining supply, find a historical entry and export. Compare against actual installed competitor flows. Public descriptions alone do not establish usability superiority.

Measure time to first successful record, unassisted completion, correction success and whether participants can explain inventory. Establish pass/fail thresholds before testing; proposed targets require confirmation, not retrofitting to results.

## Stage 2 — data core

Build schedule/history/supply consistency and offline persistence. Test edit/delete/undo, duplicate saves, manual corrections, archived containers, incompatible units and schedule revisions. Prioritize real restore and migration verification over decorative feature growth.

## Stage 3 — use over time

Add reminders, backup/recovery, observations and reports. Test notification permissions, travel/time-zone/DST behavior, historical entries, reinstall and subscription expiry. Resolve calculator and privacy scope before publishing relevant functionality.

## Stage 4 — pilot and payment

Pilot with 30–50 people for 4–6 weeks. Measure first entry, return at the user's next scheduled interval, records in at least three of the first four weeks, export/report usefulness, failures and actual payment. A weekly routine should not be judged only by daily-active-use metrics.

Test clear Free/Pro boundaries, trial and price. Measure install-to-paid, refunds, first-year receipts, support cost and acquisition cost by channel. Do not import leader revenue estimates into our forecast. Record renewal behavior when available.

## Expansion candidates

1. Widgets and supported health-platform integration.
2. CSV import with preview, source mapping, timezone handling and duplicate detection; validate competitors' available export formats first.
3. Improved reports and optional progress photos.
4. A gated deterministic calculator if permitted and validated.
5. Advanced schedules/cycles, only if user demand justifies complexity.
6. Other platforms after data portability is designed.

AI, social content and full nutrition tracking remain lower-priority options. Add only when a specific benefit is demonstrated and the routine stays usable.

## Go / revise / stop

Proceed when users repeatedly demonstrate an unmet workflow need, can use our solution independently, keep recording over time and pay despite free alternatives. Revise when people value only a one-off calculation or optional content. Stop or substantially change the proposition if there is no repeated use or viable payment/acquisition economics.

The binary recommendation in the conversation was build, not “build regardless of validation.” A small MVP/pilot is the concrete investment recommended.
