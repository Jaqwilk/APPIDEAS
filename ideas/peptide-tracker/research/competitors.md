|---|---|---|---|---|

## Prices observed in US purchase panels

- PeptidePal: monthly $9.99/$12.99/$14.99; several annual products from $19.99 to $59.99; quarterly $39.99.
- Pep AI: monthly $9.99; annual products $29.99–$49.99; weekly $3.99.
- Shotsy: monthly $9.99/$14.99/$19.99; annual $39.99/$59.99.
- PepCalc: free download, no visible IAP.

These are catalogs, not a single tested offer. Earlier search snippets sometimes showed other prices/counts; direct current listings were preferred when observations conflicted. None establish competitor revenue.

## Marketing interpretation

PeptidePal sells specialized organization and learning. Pep AI combines complex routines with wider everyday health tracking and creator-facing material. PepCalc presents a narrow task with little distraction. Shotsy emphasizes routine, progress and user understanding; its public materials also address clinicians. Website structures suggest search-content strategies, but no channel shares, advertising spend or conversion rates were established.

“What works” in this research means benefits praised in reviews, not proven causes of profit or retention.

## Corrections that matter for our plan

- PeptidePal already advertises editing, blends, advanced scheduling and Android. Trial availability changed or differs across observations; developer replies mention a three-day trial. Do not claim there is currently no trial.
- Pep AI's September redesign drew substantial criticism, but subsequent releases addressed calculator access, syringe-unit visibility and classic appearance. These are not durable standalone gaps.
- Shotsy already has multiple/custom medications, separate schedules, maintenance features, PDF, glucose and body-composition additions. A report for clinicians is not unique; provider welcome materials already exist.
- A request for an existing feature often identifies poor discoverability rather than missing capability.
- Privacy is already a marketed attribute. Differentiation needs consistent implementation, consent and portability rather than merely using the word “private.”

## Competitive opening

Proposed: a compact workflow connecting actual records with the correct supply container, visible correction/undo, and portable history. Treat this as a hypothesis to prove in task testing, not a verified exclusive feature set across the whole market.
